Standards & corrections

How we check what we publish

Fulcrum Brief covers wars, conflicts and what they cost people far from the front. Our credibility is the product, so these are the rules every briefing follows.

Sources

Every fact in a briefing comes from a named source: established news outlets, official statements and data, international bodies and think tanks. The outlet and date are shown on screen, listed in the video description and published with links on each briefing page.

A fact goes in when it is backed by a primary source (an official document, statement or dataset) or by two credible outlets. When only one outlet reports something, we say so.

Claims are labelled as claims

Governments and armies at war make claims that cannot be checked right away. We attribute them ("Kyiv says", "Moscow claims", "the Pentagon says") and never present them as established fact. Figures that cannot be tied to a date and a source stay out.

Quotes and posts, word for word

Quotes and social media posts are shown exactly as published, with the real account or outlet and the date. We do not shorten a quote in a way that changes its meaning, and a paraphrase is shown without quote marks.

Straight from the source

We go to the original documents and statements from every side of a conflict: governments, militaries, official records and their own posts, in the language they were published in. We quote them word for word, translate them faithfully, name who said it and when, and then test every claim against independent evidence. Quoting a side never means taking it.

Numbers

Prices are in US dollars unless stated, oil prices name the benchmark (Brent or WTI), and distances are in miles with kilometres in brackets. Every number carries its date, and snapshots say "as of" with the month.

Maps and footage

Our maps are built in house on public map data (Natural Earth), dated, and sourced wherever a line is contested. Footage and photos are public domain or openly licensed (for example US Department of Defense/DVIDS, Wikimedia Commons, Pixabay) and credited in each description.

We do not show casualties, graphic combat or people being harmed.

Independence and money

Fulcrum Brief is independent and not affiliated with any government, party or company. We have no sponsors at the moment; if that changes, sponsorships will be disclosed clearly in the video and the description.

Coverage of markets, savings and prices is news and analysis, never personal investment advice.

Voice

We write as analysts, not partisans: we name the actor (Washington, Moscow, Kyiv, Beijing, NATO), give each side's stated position in its own words, and keep our verdicts separate from the facts.

Corrections

If we get a fact wrong, we fix it in public. Write to us with the video, the timestamp and what is wrong. A confirmed error is corrected in the video description and on the briefing page, and logged below with the date and what changed. Corrections are never made silently.
