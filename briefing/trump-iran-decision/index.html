Trump Just Lost Congress… What Comes Next With Iran

Presented by Damyan Ivanov · Every claim checked

The briefing

Trump just lost Congress, and now he has to decide what comes next with Iran: a deal, more pressure, or new strikes. Three camps inside his own administration are fighting over it.

The Wall Street Journal reports that President Trump's advisers are split three ways on the Iran war. Vice President JD Vance and envoy Steve Witkoff want a negotiated end and the Strait of Hormuz reopened. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent want tighter sanctions and a longer blockade. Pete Hegseth, CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu are pushing for new strikes on Iran's energy, nuclear and missile sites. In this video, we break down what each camp is telling Trump, Iran's two-day plan to reopen Hormuz, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's message to the White House, and why Rubio says there's no breakthrough. We also cover the January deadline when a Democratic Congress takes over, the Houthi claims of attacks on Saudi Aramco sites and the East-West pipeline, and what all of it means for oil and gas prices. Then we lay out three realistic scenarios for what Trump does next. Every claim is checked and sourced on our website.

Section by section

How the Iran war got here: US and Israeli strikes began on February 28, Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, and the months since brought an April ceasefire, failed talks in Pakistan, a US naval blockade of Iran's ports and a June peace memo that collapsed in July. Ship traffic through Hormuz fell from about 85 ships a day to 2 on October 25, and the Pentagon put the cost of the war to the United States at 47 billion dollars in October.

The midterms and Vance's post: Democrats won both chambers of Congress on November 3. The next day Vice President JD Vance wrote, "The reason we lost is the war." Trump called that "largely true," but said he would do it again.

Camp 1, the deal makers: Vance and envoy Steve Witkoff want a negotiated end. Iran's "two-day plan," described by Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, asks Washington to end the blockade, lift sanctions and release frozen funds on day one, with Hormuz reopening on day two. Qatar is carrying messages between the two sides.

Camp 2, the squeezers: Marco Rubio and Scott Bessent want tighter sanctions. Rubio called Iran's demands "outrageous and ridiculous." Central Command says it redirected 144 commercial ships through October, and its new task force, Iron Gate, extends the blockade to Iran's land borders.

The January deadline: the new Congress takes its seats in January with the power to hold hearings and force war-powers votes, and some of Trump's advisers want any major operation launched before then.

Camp 3, the hawks: Pete Hegseth, Adm. Brad Cooper and Benjamin Netanyahu argue only force will make Iran give up its roughly 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 percent. The New York Times reports Trump has approved restarting a major campaign several times and pulled back each time.

The war is already spreading: the Houthis claimed six operations against Saudi Arabia, including the East-West pipeline that bypasses Hormuz; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed the damage. Brent fell from about 113 to 98 dollars in the week of the midterms.
