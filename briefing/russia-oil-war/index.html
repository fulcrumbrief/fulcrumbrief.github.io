Ukraine Is Setting Russia's War Money ON FIRE

Presented by Damyan Ivanov · Every claim checked

The briefing

Could Russia lose the war without losing a battle? Ukraine's long-range drones are hitting Russian oil refineries and ports, and the bill is reaching your diesel price.

In one week this November, drone strikes disrupted nine of Russia's 35 major oil refineries, about 30% of its refining capacity, according to Reuters. The Kirishi refinery near St. Petersburg burned, the Baltic port of Ust-Luga was hit, and Russia is rationing gasoline for the third time this year. In this video, we follow the drone campaign target by target, then check the money: Russia's oil and gas revenue is down 14% this year and its budget deficit is heading for nearly double the plan. But we also lay out why this is not a collapse: crude oil exports are up, the Iran war lifted oil prices, refineries get repaired, and Moscow raised taxes at home. We read what Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said in Russian about the fuel shortage, and check it against the record. Then we count what it costs Americans: record diesel prices, farm fuel bills and an $8 billion federal program. Finally, three scenarios for where this goes. Every claim is checked and sourced below.

Section by section

The drone war, strike by strike: Ukraine hit one Russian refinery every three days (International Energy Agency, Jan–Aug 2026), including Kirishi near St. Petersburg and the Baltic port of Ust-Luga. President Volodymyr Zelensky called it "a fitting response to Russia's refusal to de-escalate."

How much damage is it really? About 2.2 million barrels a day of refining, roughly 30% of capacity, was hit in one week (Reuters, 10 Nov 2026). Refinery runs fell 30% in June 2026, the lowest in more than 20 years.

Inside Russia: gasoline rationing, 8,000 tons of gasoline imported from Kazakhstan, and export bans on gasoline, jet fuel and diesel. In Moscow's words: Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak's "insignificant shortage" (RIA Novosti, 2 Oct 2026), checked against the record.

Is the money running out? Oil and gas revenue down 14.1% (Jan–Oct 2026), a 5.2 trillion ruble deficit against a 3.8 trillion plan, and why it is not a collapse: crude exports up 500,000 barrels a day and VAT raised to 22%.

What it costs you: a record U.S. diesel price of $6.52 a gallon (22 Sep 2026), farm diesel up from $3.01 to $5.61, about $2,227 more per corn farm, an $8 billion federal farm fuel program, and Washington easing sanctions on Russian diesel in October 2026.
