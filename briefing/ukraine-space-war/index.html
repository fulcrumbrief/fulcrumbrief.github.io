What Happens if Russia Gets Its Own Starlink Over Ukraine

Presented by Damyan Ivanov · Every claim checked

The briefing

What happens if Russia gets its own Starlink over Ukraine? Ukrainian intelligence says Russia's Rassvet network is already in use, and Kyiv is fighting back.

Russia has launched 32 Rassvet satellites, built by Bureau 1440 as a home-grown answer to Elon Musk's Starlink, which has more than 10,000. Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) says Rassvet is already being used for military communications over Ukraine, and that Russia plans 292 satellites by 2027. In this video, we explain what the February Starlink whitelist taught both armies, and why Ukraine hit the Progress Rocket and Space Center in Samara with Flamingo cruise missiles. We also cover what The Guardian reports about Ukrainian strikes on Roscosmos sites in Korolyov and Crimea, and how Kyiv's first test satellite, Obria Nano, built with Denmark's GomSpace, could grow into a sovereign network of around 245 satellites. Then we look at Russia's Oreshnik ballistic missile, Ukraine's three tests of exo-atmospheric interceptors, the experts who say real interception is still out of reach, and the Russian satellite Kosmos 2612 shadowing a Western radar satellite. This is the first video from Fulcrum Brief: every claim is checked and sourced below.
