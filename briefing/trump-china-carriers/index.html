Trump Is Flying to China With No US Carrier Left Near Taiwan

Presented by Damyan Ivanov · Every claim checked

The briefing

Trump is flying to China for the APEC summit with no US aircraft carrier in the western Pacific. The Iran war pulled them away, and China is watching.

President Trump heads to Shenzhen for the APEC leaders' summit on November 18–19, where he is expected to meet Xi Jinping. As of the US Naval Institute's fleet tracker dated November 9, two US Navy carriers are in the Arabian Sea and a third, USS Theodore Roosevelt, has left Singapore heading west. None is deployed near China. In this video, we trace how the Iran war drained the Pacific, starting with USS George Washington leaving Japan in August. We look at the Tomahawk and Patriot stockpile numbers reported by The Washington Post and CSIS, and at what Secretary of State Marco Rubio said about weapons for Taiwan. Then we turn to China's navy: the carriers Fujian and Liaoning, the more than 100 ships Taiwan says are at sea, and the "area denial" drills described by Taiwan's defense minister. Finally, we cover what's on the table in Shenzhen: the soybean deal and the trade truce that expires January 10, the $14 billion Taiwan arms package, Iran, and a possible meeting with Vladimir Putin that Beijing has not confirmed, including what the Kremlin says about it, in Russian. Every claim is checked and sourced on our website.

Section by section

Where the carriers went: 11 U.S. carriers, 3 sent to the Middle East and none in the western Pacific (USNI News fleet tracker, 9 Nov 2026; AP). The missile problem: more than 850 Tomahawks fired in 4 weeks.

What China's navy is doing: more than 100 Chinese ships at sea around Taiwan, the carriers Fujian and Liaoning, and "area denial" drills (Reuters).

What's on the table in Shenzhen: 25 million tons of U.S. soybeans promised and about 15 million bought, and $14 billion on hold since May. Putin, in Moscow's words: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov (RIA Novosti / Vesti, 8 Nov 2026).

More detail from the briefing:

For years America kept one carrier based in Asia, the USS George Washington in Yokosuka, Japan. When it left for the Middle East in August, the AP reported no American aircraft carrier in the western Pacific. The USS Theodore Roosevelt briefly filled the gap after sailing from San Diego in late September, training with Japan's navy and crossing the South China Sea, but it left Singapore heading west on November 9. Newsweek reports three carriers off Iran would match the biggest American carrier force in that region since 2003.

The missile problem: according to The Washington Post, US forces fired more than 850 Tomahawk cruise missiles in the first four weeks of the Iran war, and CSIS estimated in late July that most of the prewar Patriot and THAAD interceptor stock had been used. CSIS war games on Taiwan found many critical weapons would run out within weeks. Explaining a delay in weapons for Taiwan, Marco Rubio said: "If we sell you something, we don't have it anymore."

China's navy: a satellite photographed the new carrier Fujian in the South China Sea on November 2, while the Liaoning sailed in the Philippine Sea east of Taiwan. Beijing's defense ministry calls the Liaoning's training an annual routine not aimed at any country.

The soybean deal: the White House says China promised to buy 25 million tons of American soybeans a year; a Reuters count put purchases at about 15 million tons by mid-October. China still charges a 13 percent tariff on US soybeans against 3 percent on Brazilian beans, and the trade truce expires on January 10.

Three paths: the carrier gap closes quietly once the George Washington returns to Japan; Trump trades a longer truce while Taiwan's weapons stay frozen; or a new flare-up with Iran keeps all three carriers away for months.
