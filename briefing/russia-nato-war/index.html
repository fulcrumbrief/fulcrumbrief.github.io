The First 72 Hours of a Russia-NATO War: What Actually Happens?

Presented by Damyan Ivanov · Every claim checked

The briefing

If Russia attacked NATO, America would have about 72 hours to decide. We walk through the real war games, day by day, and what a Russia–NATO war would cost an American household.

This is a scenario, not a prediction, built on published war games and official documents. RAND's Baltic war games found Russian forces at the edge of Tallinn and Riga within 60 hours. A leaked German military scenario starts with cyberattacks and ends with 300,000 NATO troops moving to the eastern flank. In a war game run in December 2025 by Die Welt and Germany's military university in Hamburg, Russia took the Lithuanian city of Marijampolė in three days while the allies argued over Article 5. In this video, we explain the Suwalki Gap, Kaliningrad, what Article 5 really says, and why critics in the Baltic states reject these scenarios. We look at NATO's battlegroups from Finland to Bulgaria and Romania on the Black Sea, read what Vladimir Putin said in Russian about war with Europe, and check it against Danish intelligence. Then we count the cost for Americans: gas prices, the stock market and your 401(k), US troops in Europe, and what the law says about the draft. Every claim is checked and sourced below.

Section by section

The studies behind the scenario: the RAND Corporation's "Reinforcing Deterrence on NATO's Eastern Flank: Wargaming the Defense of the Baltics" (2016), the Bundeswehr exercise scenario reported by Euronews (January 2024), a Bloomberg Economics war-cost estimate (2025), and the Die Welt and Helmut Schmidt University war game (December 2025).

Day 3: Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty ("such action as it deems necessary, including the use of armed force"), about 15,000 Russian troops, Marijampolė in Lithuania held after three simulated days, and 72 hours of argument. Estonia's ERR News on why Baltic critics called the war game "insulting."

Day 7 and Month 1: RAND's 60 hours to Tallinn and Riga, the 7 brigades (3 heavy armor) RAND said NATO would need, and 300,000 troops in the German scenario. In Moscow's words: Vladimir Putin on war with Europe (RIA Novosti, 2 Dec 2025), checked against the Danish Defence Intelligence Service's 2025 threat assessment (6 months, 2 years, 5 years).

What it costs you: about $1.5 trillion in the first year (Bloomberg Economics), U.S. gas from $3.54 to $5.02 a gallon and Brent above $100 in 2022, the S&P 500 on 24 Feb 2022, about 68,000 U.S. troops in Europe, and the Selective Service (no draft since 1973).

3:32 Day 1: the border move

For this I'm using a war game run in December 2025 by the German paper Die Welt and the German military's university in Hamburg. Sixteen former officials and security experts played it. In that game, Russia claims there's a humanitarian crisis in Kaliningrad. Drones take control of the roads.

4:21 Day 3: would America have to fight?

So would America have to fight? Read the words. Article Five says each ally will take, quote, "such action as it deems necessary, including the use of armed force." As it deems necessary. NATO's own explanation is that each ally decides what kind of help to give.

That wording is no accident. When the treaty was drafted, the United States declined to promise it would send its forces automatically. And the article has been triggered only once in history, after the attacks of September eleventh, 2001. So in the war game, the allies had to choose.

Here's what the players did. The United States declined to trigger Article Five, because of the humanitarian cover story. Germany hesitated. Poland mobilized, but its troops stopped at the mined border.

6:17 Day 7: the 60-hour war game

But what if it isn't a small grab? What if it's the big one? This is the famous study. Between 2014 and 2015, the RAND Corporation ran a series of war games on a full Russian invasion of the Baltic states.

Across all those games, the longest it took Russian forces to reach the outskirts of Tallinn or Riga, the capitals of Estonia and Latvia, was sixty hours. Two and a half days. RAND's verdict was blunt: as it was then positioned, NATO could not successfully defend its most exposed members. About seven brigades, three of them heavy armor, already in place before the shooting starts. A brigade is a few thousand soldiers. Bloomberg's 2025 scenario adds the sea. In its wider version, Russia's fleet moves to control the Baltic Sea while ground forces close the Suwalki Gap.
